# The scoring variant is chosen by how it separates the listener's yes from their no, and re-chosen at every batch

The eval chooses a scoring variant by how well it ranks the offers the listener kept (a Rating of 3★ or more, or liked without a Rating; weighted by stars) above the offers they turned down (a Pass, a Dislike, an expiry after a play, a removal by hand). The measure is the AUC, the chance that a kept offer outscores a turned-down one, with each kept offer left out of its own positives. A variant whose top 50 pool scores span less than 0.25 pool-score sd is degenerate (its top picks are near-ties) and is not chosen unless every variant is. Within 0.02 AUC the simpler variant wins. The first batch stores the choice. Every later batch re-runs the eval before scoring and switches only when the current variant is degenerate or the winner's AUC is more than 0.03 higher. Until there are 15 kept and 15 turned-down offers with a preview vector, the eval uses the rule before it (the mean percentile of held-out liked songs among the pool) and never switches. Each run is a row in `eval_runs`.

The old rule measured whether old likes outrank random pool tracks. On the reference installation it chose `genre` at a mean percentile of 59.4 (50 = chance; the other variants 50.9 to 59.8). Under `genre` the listener's yes tracks ranked at a mean percentile of 47 and their no tracks at 54, and its top 30 scores spanned 0.908 to 0.936. Of the third batch's 16 decided picks from the top of the ranking, 3 were rated 3★ or more and 8 were Disliked.

Both sides are offers because both were picked from the top of the pool the same way. Held-out liked songs were never picked, and against turned-down offers they make whichever variant did the picking look worst. On the reference installation that comparison gave AUCs of 0.12 to 0.45, while kept against turned-down offers gave 0.50 to 0.63.

**Considered Options**: keep the mean percentile of held-out liked songs (it measures familiarity and can't see a no); held-out liked songs as the AUC's positives, as first planned (the selection bias above); re-choosing only by hand with `eval --choose` (the choice stays frozen at the first batch while verdicts grow); switching to every new winner (with 15 to 20 offers a side the AUC's sd is about 0.1, so the choice would flap on noise).

**Consequences**: a fresh installation has no offers and chooses as before until it has 15 of each. With a few dozen verdicts, AUC differences under about 0.1 are noise: the tie and the switch margin damp flapping but don't make a switch significant. The objective sees only what was offered, so it judges the top of the ranking, where a batch comes from, and says nothing about songs no variant ranks high. Every batch spends a few seconds on the eval first. The eval scores without the negative penalty, so a turned-down offer isn't pushed down by its own verdict.
