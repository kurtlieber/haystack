# A possible cover is flagged, and the listener may swap it for its Original

Spotify's search, radio and "fans also like" turn up covers that pass for the real thing: a whole album of soundalike covers of other artists' hits, a karaoke version, a tribute band, a live take. The listener rates them in needle without noticing, so the cover gets the heart and the playlist slot meant for the song, and the re-ranker learns from a recording the listener never meant to choose. A cleanup of Rock Rock Rock and the other playlists on 2026-10-23 removed 62 such covers by hand.

That cleanup measured four signs. A **marker** in the title or artist: cover, karaoke, tribute, "originally performed by", "made famous by", "in the style of". A **live** take: "Live at", Unplugged, a BBC session. A **borrowed ISRC**: Deezer files the track's ISRC under another artist's track of the same title, which happens when a cover label reuses the original's code. A **rank gap**: another artist's track of the same title is at least three times as popular on Deezer. Together they found 57 of the 62 covers. They flagged none of 19 famous covers whose cover is the better-known recording (Hurt, All Along the Watchtower, Jolene by the White Stripes), because the rank gap only flags a track much less popular than another artist's. They also flagged about 14% of the other tracks, mostly songs that share a title with a bigger hit.

14% is too many to act on alone, so the signs are a hint. From 0.9.0 the API checks each track once, in the background: the next 20 in each queue and in an opened playlist, and any card needle asks for, ahead of those. Each check takes up to three Deezer calls on its shared throttle, so a whole playlist is never queued at once. A check that fails is tried again a day later. A track card carries `original` (feature `card.original`): the Original's title and artist, its Spotify id when a search finds it, and the sign. needle shows it as a badge, and the listener decides.

When they decide it is a cover, needle sends a **Swap** (feature `swaps`, `POST /v1/swaps`). The cover's Verdicts are taken back, kept aside so a mistaken Swap can be undone by hand. In each playlist the listener owns that holds the cover, the Original goes where the cover was, unless its Song is there already, and the cover comes out. A heart on the cover moves to the Original, unless the Original's Song was rated below 3★. If haystack put the cover on To Rip, it comes off, and the Original goes on in its place on the same terms, unless it is in the library already.

**Playlists.** Until now haystack never edited the listener's own playlists: it wrote only Liked Songs, Discoveries and To Rip. A Swap is the exception, and only because the listener asks for it on that track. It touches only playlists the listener owns, never followed ones, and reads each playlist from Spotify before it writes, so it works from the playlist as it is now, at Spotify's positions, which count the local files haystack doesn't store. Discoveries is left alone: a cover there is passed or rated like any offer.

**Considered Options**: removing covers automatically (a 14% false-flag rate would take real songs out of the listener's playlists); blocking cover artists (a cover label releases under a new artist name for each album, and blocking a live take's artist would block the band); asking a language model to judge each flag (it might cut the false flags, but it costs a call per flagged track and its judgement is no better than the listener's, who is already looking at the card); fixing it in needle alone (needle can't search Deezer, and the Swap's Spotify writes belong with haystack's other writes and their retries).

**Consequences**: a cover better known than its original is not flagged, by design, and neither is a cover that never says so and has no more popular namesake: the listener still has to catch those. A song that shares its title with a bigger hit is flagged, and its badge points at the wrong song; the listener ignores it. A Swap can't be undone from needle. The taken-back Verdicts are in `swap_retracted`, and the playlist edit has to be undone in Spotify. The cover's plays stay, so it still counts in the Baseline's play history. An older needle ignores `original` and has no Swap.
