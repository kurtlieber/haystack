# Traits are measured on the server from Deezer previews

Spotify's development-mode Web API (2026) has no audio-features endpoint, so needle's Readings for the seven Traits come from the server: Essentia models run on the same 30 s Deezer preview the harvest already decodes. The discovery classifiers (danceability, acousticness, instrumentalness) reuse the `discogs-effnet-bs64-1` backbone pass that the genre styles already need; valence and energy come from a valence/arousal regression model (energy is the arousal estimate); tempo from beat tracking; loudness from EBU R128 over the clip. The listener's liked songs are measured the same way from their previews, so a track's Reading and their Baseline are comparable. Tracks the server has never seen are measured on demand when needle asks for them.

Readings are model estimates from a 30 s clip, not Spotify's numbers, and a track with no Deezer preview has no Readings (about 8% of liked songs in 2026-10).

**Considered Options**: ReccoBeats (Spotify-lookalike values keyed by Spotify id; a third party whose values can't be checked, with coverage gaps; already ruled out for discovery); measuring the playing audio on the desktop (whole track, but TensorFlow on the desktop the server exists to spare, and Python dependencies on every desktop).

## Note (2026-10-11): tempo takes Deezer's count when it agrees

On 20 liked songs, beat tracking on the clip (RhythmExtractor2013, and TempoCNN as a check) matched the bpm Deezer publishes for the track within 4% 16 times, and up to an octave all 20 times: the misses counted drum & bass at 85 instead of 170. So the tempo Reading is Deezer's bpm when it is the same tempo as the measurement at some metrical level (x2, x1/2, x3/2, x2/3), and the measurement otherwise (Deezer often has no bpm). Valence and energy use `deam-msd-musicnn-2`: no valence/arousal head exists for the effnet backbone, so they need one extra, small `msd-musicnn-1` pass.

## Note (2026-10-20): drum & bass and jungle tempos are folded by style

Deezer's bpm rescued less than the 2026-10-11 note assumed. Only `/track/{id}` (and `/track/isrc:`) carry a `bpm`: the track objects that search, charts, an artist's top tracks or radio and albums return have no such key, so every new candidate and every liked song found by a text search was measured with no hint at all. And Deezer has a bpm (not 0) for only about a third of tracks (65 of 200 sampled). The hint is now fetched wherever it was missing, at one more throttled request per new candidate.

On one library's 3,092 Readings, 79 tracks had Drum n Bass or Jungle as their top style: 27 read 155 or more, 42 under 100 (half time) and 10 between 100 and 155, mostly near 116 (two thirds: the beat tracker locks onto a triplet feel). One full-length track at 172 read 172, 117 and 87 in three 30 s windows, and 115 from its preview. So when the clip's top style is Drum n Bass or Jungle, at an activation of 0.3 or more, a tempo outside 155-190 is doubled, or else multiplied by 3/2, when that lands inside. The fold runs after Deezer's bpm is taken, since Deezer too counts some drum & bass at 86, and on stored Readings each harvest (no audio needed). On that library it moved 43 tempos. Below 0.3 the style guess is too weak: an indie song at 118, by Deezer and the clip alike, had a top style of Drum n Bass at 0.21.

**Considered Options**: the same fold for Halftime, Dubstep, Trap and Hardcore (each is played at both levels: Halftime split between 80-90 and 170-180, Dubstep between 70-90 and 140-150, Hardcore from 90 to 180 with slowed edits, so no range is right for all of them; they stay as measured); TempoCNN (the 2026-10-11 check found the same octave choices); measuring a liked song's own file instead of its preview (only library songs have one, and a Reading would stop being comparable with the preview-measured candidates). A drum & bass track whose clip the style head hears as another style stays unfolded, like the full-length track above (its preview's top style is Hardcore): that is the known gap.
